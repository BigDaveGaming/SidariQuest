BIG DAVE & GOBBY SHARON'S SIDARI QUEST
"Learn Greek. Cause Chaos."
A Big Dave Gaming production
Game concept — v2 (settled decisions)

==================================================
1. THE PITCH
==================================================

Big Dave and Gobby Sharon from Bootle have rented an apartment in Sidari,
on the north coast of Corfu, for the WHOLE SUMMER (May to September).
They are "that couple": loud Brits abroad who think speaking English
slowly and loudly counts as Greek. It has stopped working.

Dave booked it thinking it was all-inclusive. It's self-catering.
Now they have to learn Greek just to eat.

Sidari Quest is an ad-free, offline phone game (PWA) that teaches
practical, everyday Modern Greek — what you actually say on holiday in
Greece. Each lesson is a real situation; the right Greek gets you out of a
Dave-or-Sharon disaster, the wrong answer gets a cringe moment.

The joke: the game teaches you to be LESS like them.

==================================================
2. PLAYING AS DAVE OR SHARON
==================================================

You choose Big Dave or Gobby Sharon. Your choice decides whether the game
teaches the male or female forms of Greek (Dave: "είμαι κουρασμένος",
Sharon: "είμαι κουρασμένη" — I'm tired), and changes the jokes and
reactions you get.

3. THE CHARACTERS
==================================================

BIG DAVE
50s, bald, sunburnt head, grey stubble, sunglasses on head. Palm-tree
vest, gold chain, gold watch, tribal tattoos, shorts, white socks,
sliders. Confident about everything, right about very little. Can't be
without a white van even on holiday: hired one at the airport and slapped
on the Union Jack magnets he packed specially.

GOBBY SHARON
40s/50s, copper-ginger messy bun, drawn-on brows, huge lashes, big lips,
gold hoops, leopard-print bikini top, leopard phone case, phone always in
hand. Talks to everyone, never stops, calls every waiter "darlin'".
400 posts in the Sidari Expats Facebook group; left a one-star review
because the taverna had no chips. Sharon's selfie spot: the Canal d'Amour.

Unlockable outfit: "Just arrived from Bootle" (Dave in a paint-splattered
navy hoodie, Sharon in a black puffer gilet — in 30°C heat).

THE LOCALS — the Spyros running joke
Spyros is hugely common on Corfu (the island's patron saint is Saint
Spyridon), so roughly every fourth Greek man is a Spyros, told apart by
nickname. Each one teaches an adjective. Dave and Sharon can never keep
them straight.

- Kyria Eleni — the landlady. Yiayia, brings figs, sees everything.
- Kyrios Spyros (ο κύριος Σπύρος / "Κύριε Σπύρο!") — the landlord, Eleni's
  husband. The only Spyros they're respectful to, because he holds the
  deposit. Teaches formal address.
- Spyros Rent-a-Van — the van hire man. Inspects every new scratch.
- Μεγάλος Σπύρος (Big Spyros) — the taverna. Huge, endlessly patient.
- Μικρός Σπύρος (Little Spyros) — the baker. Tiny, up at 6am.
- Χοντρός Σπύρος (Fat Spyros) — the mini market. Lives behind the till.
- Γέρο-Σπύρος (Old Spyros) — outside the kafeneio, judging.
- Ψηλός Σπύρος (Tall Spyros) — the taxi driver, folded into a tiny car.
- Katerina — the pharmacist. Has seen every sunburn.
- The cat — a stray that steals Dave's chips. Pure chaos.

4. SIDARI (researched)
==================================================

- Small village (around 400 residents), a fishing village until 1980s
  tourism.
- The Strip: the busy main road behind the main beach, lined with British
  pubs, big-screen football, bingo, karaoke, tribute acts, full English
  breakfasts everywhere, gift shops and supermarkets open late.
- Main beach: long, sandy, shallow, Blue Flag; beach cafés with their own
  sunbeds (buy a drink, get a sunbed); banana boats, pedalos.
- Old fishing harbour: boat hire.
- Souvenir market: olive-wood bowls, handmade shoes, knick-knacks.
- Canal d'Amour: golden sandstone cliffs and coves. Scenery/selfie spot
  ONLY — no swimming-the-channel jokes (it's genuinely dangerous).
- Sidari Water Park.
- Bus: KTEL "green bus" to Corfu Town (about an hour) — it's a plain white
  European coach, not green. Dave waits for a green bus while three white
  coaches go past. Lesson: read the sign, not the colour.
- Day trips: Sunset Beach (Logas) at Peroulades — 66 steps; Corfu Town;
  ferries to the Diapontia islands (Ereikoussa, Mathraki, Othonoi).

6. HOW THE GAME WORKS
==================================================

EVERY LESSON = TEACH, THEN PRACTISE
1. Phrasebook first: before the scene, Dave/Sharon's phrasebook teaches
   the 5–6 phrases for that lesson, one card at a time — Greek with audio
   and Latin letters, meaning, word-by-word breakdown, a tip, then an easy
   check straight after each card.
2. The scene: use what you've just learned with the locals. Each phrase
   has a 📖 hint button the first time it appears.
3. Anything you get wrong comes back at the end of the lesson until you
   get it right.

- Map of Sidari: places unlock as you progress; small May–September
  progress bar on top.
- Scenes: location background + cut-out characters whose faces change
  with your answers (Dave/Sharon: normal, proud, confused, cringe,
  fuming, shocked, talking; locals: normal, pleased, unimpressed).
- Question types: what does this mean, how do you say it, listen and
  choose, build the sentence, what do you reply, read the sign, price
  check.
- Greek on signs is added by the game (always correct), and doubles as
  reading practice.
- Spaced repetition, optional transliteration, slow audio, male/female
  forms based on your character, formal vs informal explained.
- Rewards: figs from Kyria Eleni; streak = "days without embarrassing
  yourself"; achievements ("Parked Legally", "Ordered Without Pointing",
  "Kyria Eleni Smiled").
